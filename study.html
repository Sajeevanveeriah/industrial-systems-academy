01 / Material flow

Follow the potatoes

Start with the product. At each machine ask what goes in, what changes and where each output goes.

Receiving ties the delivery to a lot, an identifiable group of material. Washing removes soil and stones. Peeling removes skin, and cutting makes strips. Blanching treats the strips in hot water. After drying, the fryer removes more water and adds some oil to the product.

The freezer changes the strips’ temperature. The packer produces complete bags, and dispatch transfers them to a delivery. The training model leaves loose carryover at packing. A real plant uses its approved product and process specifications.

Worked diagnosis

The washer takes in 1,000 kg, but only 980 kg reaches the peeler. The washer’s debris record shows 20 kg. Both streams total 1,000 kg, so the first transfer balances. If that debris record were missing, you would have an unexplained difference to investigate.

Checkpoint: Which record explains a drop in weight at the peeler?

Compare the peeled product and the peel-removal stream from the same lot and time interval. A start command alone cannot explain the weight change. Compare the two measured material streams.

02 / Industrial IT

Read a network drawing

A physical drawing helps you find a cable or port. A logical drawing shows which systems are intended to communicate. An application test tells you whether they do.

A switch forwards frames between network ports. Fibre carries light between its transceivers. Copper Ethernet carries electrical signals. The drawing’s legend and endpoint names tell you which connection to inspect.

A logical network can divide devices into VLANs, which group traffic on the switches. Communication between groups also depends on routing, an allowed traffic policy and an application listening at the expected address.

Worked diagnosis

An operator display loses every controller value after a server move. The server’s copper port shows link after the move. The separate fibre trunk between the switches also shows carrier. The old server port used the controls VLAN, while the new port belongs to the office VLAN. Compare the intended port assignment with the recorded switch configuration, then verify an authorised read from the display.

Checkpoint: A link light is on. What has that established?

The network interfaces detect a physical link. You still need evidence of the correct VLAN, address, route, application access and a fresh value. A named application read tests more of the path than the link light.

03 / Controls and supervision

Follow a sensor signal

One photoelectric sensor can tell a controller that a carton is present. Following that one signal makes the larger control system easier to understand.

The programmable logic controller (PLC) samples inputs and executes its program. A signal held high across several scans can represent one carton. Counting the transition from clear to blocked records its arrival once.

The human-machine interface (HMI) is the operator screen. SCADA supervises and collects equipment data. A historian stores selected observations. Its sampling and storage settings determine which events a later trend can show.

Worked diagnosis

The photoeye indication and the PLC tag change, but the HMI still says “clear”. Its source timestamp is old and quality is stale. Follow the gateway-to-screen path and check the binding. Treat the last value as historical until a fresh sample arrives.

Checkpoint: The input is high for three scans. How many arrivals?

If the previous sample was low and the input remained high, the trace contains one rising edge. Counting every high scan would report three arrivals. A pulse that falls entirely between samples poses a different problem: the controller may miss it.

04 / Manufacturing operations

Release and schedule a lot

Receiving proves what arrived. Quality determines whether that lot can be used. A production schedule reserves the time and equipment to run it.

A loaded vehicle weighs 5,200 kg. Its empty weight, or tare, is 4,200 kg. Subtracting tare gives 1,000 kg of incoming potatoes. Match the delivery, scale ticket and quality sample to the same lot before issuing the material.

A manufacturing execution system (MES) records production against the order and lot. Advanced planning and scheduling (APS) checks whether the eligible line has enough time. At 600 kg/h, the 1,000 kg charge occupies 100 minutes of run time.

Worked diagnosis

A planner reserves the line from 08:00 to 09:40 and expects to finish on time. The preceding product requires a 20-minute changeover. Starting setup at 08:00 pushes completion to 10:00, fifteen minutes late. Beginning setup at 07:40 resolves the conflict if that slot is free.

Checkpoint: Can a free line use a lot still on quality hold?

The scheduler can show available capacity. Material release is another condition: the lot needs an accepted quality result, correct identity and sufficient quantity before it can enter this training order.

05 / Traceability and ERP

Reconcile the shipment

Trace the same batch through two sets of records: the process weights and the inventory movements.

Enterprise resource planning (ERP) records business transactions. In the simulated SAP ledger, a 101 receipt brings the raw lot into stock. A 261 issue transfers those potatoes into production. Another 261 records 37.866 kg of absorbed oil. The process model also records 13.334 kg of water uptake.

Packing posts a 101 receipt for 744 kg of finished stock. Dispatch posts a 601 issue for that same 744 kg. If this batch was the only finished stock, those two transactions leave zero finished stock on hand. The production record still shows the 744 kg produced.

Worked diagnosis

The dispatch interface times out after sending the 601 movement. The timeout alone cannot establish whether the receiving system posted it. Find the original event ID, inspect its status and replay using the same identity. A second delivery must leave the booked quantity at 744 kg.

Checkpoint: Why can a 101 movement appear twice for one batch?

The material and business event distinguish them. One 101 records a receipt of raw potatoes. The later 101 records newly packed finished goods. Each has its own event ID. Repeating the finished receipt with the same event ID should leave its stock effect unchanged.

Film transcript

The batch walkthrough

1. One lot, two identities

A truck delivers one thousand kilograms of potatoes. The supplier calls this lot POT-2601. The plant assigns production batch FRY-1001. Keep both identifiers: one identifies the incoming material, the other the work performed. We will follow that link from receiving through control, packing and inventory reconciliation.

2. Release before scheduling

The receiving operator matches the delivery to its purchase reference and checks gross weight minus tare against the measured net mass. The manufacturing execution system, or MES, records the supplier lot on hold. A matching inspection result and confirmed put-away release the material. Advanced planning and scheduling, or APS, can then reserve line capacity for that released order. A reservation does not start the conveyor.

3. Follow the physical path

The physical plan answers "where?" It locates the detector and the controller input at the packing cell. Check the actual connection at the equipment. The logical view asks "what must communicate?" The controller sends the carton-present tag through a switch to the supervisory server. A diagram line does not prove the cable, the received optical light level or a healthy application. Check the link and optical reading, then verify tag value, quality and sample time.

4. Command and feedback

The controller requests conveyor motion. A run command means the program asked for motion. The drive feedback tells us what actually happened. With command on and feedback off, the operator checks the permissives and the drive status before assuming the belt moved. The supervisory control and data acquisition system, or SCADA, displays both signals. The historian records their time sequence.

5. Stale is different from zero

Look at two samples. The first detector reads zero, with good quality and a current timestamp. That is evidence of no carton at that sample. The second display also shows zero, but its timestamp has stopped advancing. The current detector state is unknown. Compare the source and server timestamps, communication quality and last change. A steady process can still produce fresh samples.

6. Reconcile the packed mass

The packing scale records seven hundred and forty-four kilograms of finished fries against FRY-1001. That is a packed-to-raw ratio of seventy-four point four per cent. The difference is not automatically scrap. Reconcile the stage records: potato input plus measured additions must equal packed output plus recorded removals. MES keeps the lot genealogy.

7. Replay once, count once

The enterprise resource planning system, SAP, receives the packed-stock posting. Suppose the acknowledgement is lost after the posting succeeds. The interface retries the same event identifier and quantity. The receiver recognises the original posting and returns its acknowledgement. Inventory remains seven hundred and forty-four kilograms. Verify one movement, one event key, and a trace from finished batch back to supplier lot.
