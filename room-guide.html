Before equipment arrives

Survey the service bay

Ask what must survive

Agree with operations which services must run during a short outage, which can shut down, how much history can be lost and who can accept an interruption. Record the upstream dependencies: a local server staying on does not keep an unprotected upstream switch alive.

Measure the room, not just the rack

Record door and delivery-route dimensions, rack depth including connectors, service access at both ends, floor capacity, water and dust exposure, cooling supply and return, access control and after-hours alarm ownership. Obtain the rail, loading and clearance limits for each selected device.

Release gate Keep a dimensioned plan, dependency list, asset schedule and agreed constraints. Facilities and qualified trades own structural suitability, fixed wiring, protective devices, earthing, fire protection and cooling installation. Resolve gaps before equipment is energised.

Worked design

Size for power, heat and time

The following loads and margin are training assumptions, not equipment ratings. For a real build, record measured peak W and VA at representative load, planned additions, start-up behaviour and manufacturer limits. A dual-supply server is one load, not two copies of its nameplate capacity.

Peak IT load600 WHost 460 W + switch 100 W + edge device 40 W. Assume all are in-room loads.
Design load750 W600 W × 1.25. At a conservative aggregate power factor of 0.90, allow about 834 VA (750 / 0.90, rounded up).
IT heat at design load2,559 BTU/h750 W × 3.412. Add UPS losses and other room heat; account separately for loads powered here but dissipating heat elsewhere.

Read the runtime curve

For comparison, Eaton’s base 5PX1500IRT2UAUG2 row is for a 1,500 W unit. The AU/NZ runtime chart lists 14 minutes at 50% and 11 minutes at 60% for its stated VA = W load condition. The 750 W design point is 50% of rated watts. A non-unity power factor is not the chart condition: confirm both output ratings and obtain manufacturer expectations for the real load and battery configuration.

Decision If the example needs 5 minutes to ride through an interruption and 4 minutes to shut down, the 14-minute chart point suggests 5 minutes of nominal margin, not a guarantee. Agree a reserve for battery age and temperature. Witness an approved non-production runtime test under representative load; retain load, battery condition, timing and shutdown logs. Physical installation

Plan a 12U elevation

Start at the floor

This is one arrangement, not a universal equipment list. Start at U1: trained installers secure the cabinet and use the correct rated rails and lifting arrangements for the heaviest unit, then fit the host above it. Confirm that the actual chassis, rails and vertical power distribution unit (PDU) fit with the doors shut.

Follow the air

Place the rack intake towards the cold aisle in the service bay and exhaust towards the return. Check each appliance’s fan direction rather than assuming every switch draws air through its front. Close unused front spaces with blanks; keep cables clear of fans. Trend intake temperatures at bottom, middle and top during representative peak operation, comparing each with its installed device limit.

Trace a complete path

Connect and label both ends

Power path

Approved supply → UPS01 → rack PDU → SRV01 and SW01

In the simulator the PDU’s server and switch leads are already connected. In the room, record the source, outlet and device inlet at both ends of every lead. Example: R01-PDU01-03 / SRV01-PSU1. If both power supplies share this UPS, record the common failure point. Two cords do not establish independent feeds.

Data path

Upstream termination P01 → switch uplink → access port → SRV01

The patch panel is passive. Patch its P01 front jack to the switch uplink; record where the permanent link behind P01 terminates. Connect a distinct switch access port to the server NIC. Record both port IDs, media, transceiver compatibility and cable test result. Label both ends; do not infer purpose from colour.

Acceptance Match the installed port map to the elevation and asset labels. At both ends verify the agreed link speed, VLAN carriage and error-counter baseline. A link light proves neither the VLAN nor a working application.

From metal to application

Configure the host and its services

Training host: 10.42.8.20/24 · Gateway: 10.42.8.1 · DNS: 10.42.8.10 · Access VLAN: 42

  1. Network. Reserve the address and record the MAC and switch port. For this untagged server interface, assign access VLAN 42; ensure the upstream link carries VLAN 42 in the agreed mode at both ends. Apply the /24 prefix, gateway and internal resolver. Confirm the expected record using the configured resolver, then test a permitted application session across the intended boundary. A VLAN is not an access rule.
  2. Host. Capture firmware and storage configuration, usable capacity, network bindings, management access and clock source. Set boot and service restart behaviour deliberately. Restrict management to named administrators on the approved management path. Keep the recovery account under the site’s credential process.
  3. Virtual machines. Map roles before assigning CPU, memory or disk: directory/DNS provides identity and names; SCADA supervises the process; the historian records time-series data. Avoid making the host’s only recovery procedure depend on a guest it cannot start. Keep backups in a separate failure domain. A single host remains a single point of failure across all its guests.
  4. Bring-up. Restore network, name and time dependencies first, then databases and dependent applications. From an operator client, resolve the service name and read back a timestamped, good-quality test point. Record clock offset against the agreed tolerance and verify an unauthorised test role does not have write access.

Commissioning dossier

Prove the installed result

A factory acceptance test (FAT) proves the prepared build on a bench. A site acceptance test (SAT) proves the installed path. Record the configuration revision, witness, timestamp, actual result and evidence for each. An unrun test is open, not passed.

FAT / Normal boot

Start the isolated build from its documented state. Pass if the intended service name resolves and the operator account reads the known test point after a restart. Keep the DNS result and application readback.

SAT / End-to-end event

Use an approved test point on the installed path. Pass if its value, quality, source time and identity agree at SCADA and in historical readback. Keep evidence from both ends and the as-built port schedule.

SAT / Degraded service

In an approved test window or isolated test rig, use the manufacturer’s supported test procedure. Confirm monitored battery mode, operator notification and agreed service behaviour. Do not improvise a mains interruption. Keep event and acknowledgement times and any qualified witness report.

Restore / Business service

Example acceptance targets: restore within 60 minutes and lose no more than 15 minutes of data. Restore an independent copy onto an isolated network. Time to a successful historical query is the recovery time; the gap to the newest recovered sample measures data loss. Record both, not just “VM booted”.

Handover

Leave the room maintainable

Give the owner the elevation, physical and logical diagrams, asset and software revisions, power and port schedules, backups, restore procedure and signed evidence. Record unresolved deviations with an owner and a retest criterion.

What should be monitored?

Trend intake temperature, UPS load and battery health, disk headroom, storage faults, link errors, time drift, application freshness and backup age. Give each alert an owner and escalation path; demonstrate one notification reaches a person through a channel that survives the fault.

What does a maintenance cycle include?

Agree risk-based intervals and named owners. Review trends and capacity; exercise a restore; check alert routing; review access; stage supported updates in an isolated copy; keep a rollback and retest plan. Follow device instructions for battery inspection and replacement. Update diagrams and recovery records after every relevant change.
